Does it make financial sense?

Every idea is scored in money: what it costs, and what it gives back in commuters' time, buses no longer needed and running costs saved. Costs come from published LTA/MOT figures where they exist. Where they don't, the number is labelled an assumption and can be changed in costs.json.

1 · New lines and stations: benefit vs cost

Benefit = minutes saved by newly served residents (valued at half the median wage) + feeder buses no longer needed, over years. A ratio above 1.0 means the benefits outweigh the cost. Land value uplift is shown separately as a way to fund the project, not added to the benefit, since it is largely the same benefit showing up in home prices.

3 · More trains on the most crowded stretches

For each line: the busiest stretch in the rush hour, how full its busiest 15 minutes get, and how many extra trains would keep it under the target.

4 · Bus-to-MRT hotspots: infill station or express bus?

For the 15 biggest clusters of people bussing to the MRT each morning: an infill station on a nearby line (only possible if the line passes within 800 m) vs a direct express bus, both over the full appraisal period.

6 · Trains running nearly empty

Hours when even the fullest stretch of a line is under 25% full. Running trains a little less often (never more than 8 minutes apart) saves running cost, minus the value of riders' extra waiting.
